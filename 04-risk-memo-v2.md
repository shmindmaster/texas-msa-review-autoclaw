# Risk Memo — Bluebonnet / Pecan Creek Master Services Agreement

**To:** Operations Lead  |  **From:** Contract review (Texas commercial contracts)  |  **Date:** November 19, 2026
**Re:** Master Services Agreement dated November 1, 2026 between Bluebonnet Field Services LLC and Pecan Creek Logistics, Inc.

> **This is a product-demo fixture.** The parties, the matter, and the agreement are fictional. This memo is not legal advice, creates no attorney-client relationship, and must not be relied on for any real transaction. Anything uncertain or high-stakes is listed for a licensed Texas attorney.

## Summary

The agreement locks Bluebonnet into two years of work at $18,000 a month and then takes almost everything else away. Payment can be withheld for any reason or no reason, with no interest. Bluebonnet's liability is unlimited while the customer's is capped at one month's fees. Bluebonnet must indemnify the customer even for the customer's own negligence, in small plain type buried in the middle of a long paragraph. Bluebonnet and its technicians are barred from working for any logistics company anywhere in Texas for three years after the job ends, with no tie to confidential information. The customer may feed Bluebonnet's service data into AI systems with no consent and no disclosure. And the whole contract is written under Delaware law with lawsuits confined to New York, for two Texas companies doing Texas work.

Texas law will not repair most of this: it enforces the contract as written and implies no duty of good faith to soften it. Two clauses are also likely unenforceable in the customer's favor — which is not a win for Bluebonnet, because an uncollectible indemnity and an unenforceable non-compete leave both sides worse off than a fair clause would have.

## Needs a licensed Texas attorney

1. **Whether the Texas construction anti-indemnity statute applies.** It voids indemnity for a party's own negligence or fault in construction contracts and collateral agreements, where the indemnitor has insurance subject to the statute. "Construction contract" includes maintenance of an improvement to real property, so maintenance of movable equipment is probably outside it; built-in building systems may not be. [Tex. Ins. Code §§ 151.001(5), 151.101, 151.102]
2. **Whether the fair-notice rule reaches limitation-of-liability clauses.** The Texas Supreme Court applied fair notice to indemnities and releases for a party's own negligence, and declined to extend it to a clause allocating contract damages. No controlling decision was located that squarely decides whether it applies to a liability cap in a services contract, and some courts of appeals have applied it. [Dresser, 853 S.W.2d 505 (Tex. 1993); Green Int'l, Inc. v. Solis, 951 S.W.2d 384 (Tex. 1997)]
3. **Whether a non-compete in a company-to-company services agreement is measured the same way as an employee covenant.** Chapter 15 applies to covenants not to compete generally, but the reported decisions are mostly about employees or sales of a business. [Tex. Bus. & Com. Code § 15.50(a)]
4. **Whether the Texas Responsible Artificial Intelligence Governance Act is triggered** by the intended AI use, including its prohibitions, disclosure duties, and small-business exceptions. [Tex. Bus. & Com. Code chs. 551–554]
5. **Whether the Texas Data Privacy and Security Act reaches personal data in the service data.** It exempts individuals acting in a commercial or employment context, so employee data is largely outside it; consumer data captured in the file is not. [Tex. Bus. & Com. Code ch. 541]
6. **The federal Defend Trade Secrets Act immunity notice** that should accompany any confidentiality clause. [18 U.S.C. § 1833(b)]
7. **Usury.** Any interest rate on overdue invoices must fit the Texas Finance Code, starting with the general 10% ceiling and the optional rate ceilings. [Tex. Fin. Code § 302.001(b); Tex. Fin. Code ch. 303 ("Optional Rate Ceilings")]
