# Redline — Bluebonnet Field Services LLC / Pecan Creek Logistics, Inc. MSA

**What this is.** A clause-by-clause redline of the fictional Master Services Agreement in `01-fictional-msa.md`, limited to the clauses the Texas baseline playbook (`02-texas-baseline-playbook.md`) flags. Nothing else in the agreement is touched.

**How to read it.** Deletions are shown as ~~struck text~~ and additions as **bold text**. Each item states the original text, the proposed text, and a reviewer comment giving severity, the playbook rule, and the citation. The same changes appear as Microsoft Word tracked changes in `03-redline.docx`.

**Who this is for.** The brief asks for a careful Texas commercial-contracts reviewer for a non-lawyer operations lead at a small Texas company. The planted defects in `01-fictional-msa.md` are all drafted in the customer's favor, so the flags below run mainly in favor of Bluebonnet Field Services LLC (the provider), while two of them — the unenforceable indemnity and the unenforceable non-compete — also leave the customer with less than it thinks it bought. The recommended action on each item states which side must move. This is a product-demo fixture, not a negotiation position.

## R-1. Section 11 — Non-Competition

**Severity:** High
**Playbook rule:** NCO-1, NCO-2, NCO-3
**Citation:** Tex. Bus. & Com. Code §§ 15.50(a), 15.51(c), 15.52; Marsh USA Inc. v. Cook, 354 S.W.3d 764 (Tex. 2011)

**Original text (Section 11.1–11.2):**

> During the term of this Agreement and for a period of three (3) years following the expiration or termination of this Agreement for any reason, neither Provider nor any of its Personnel shall, directly or indirectly, as an owner, partner, shareholder, employee, consultant, contractor, investor, or otherwise, provide services to, or engage in the business of, any logistics company, warehousing company, distribution company, or third-party maintenance provider serving any such company, anywhere in the State of Texas.
>
> Provider acknowledges and agrees that the covenants in this Section 11 are given without any consideration beyond the fees payable under this Agreement and are not tied to the disclosure or receipt of any confidential information, trade secret, or customer relationship.

**Proposed text:**

> During the term of this Agreement and for a period of ~~three (3) years~~ **twelve (12) months** following the expiration or termination of this Agreement for any reason, neither Provider nor any of its Personnel shall, directly or indirectly, as an owner, partner, shareholder, employee, consultant, contractor, investor, or otherwise, provide services to, or engage in the business of, any logistics company, warehousing company, distribution company, or third-party maintenance provider serving any such company, anywhere in the State of Texas**; provided, that this Section 11 restrains Provider and its Personnel only with respect to the equipment maintenance programs performed under this Agreement and only within the counties in which Customer then operates a facility subject to a Statement of Work, and does not prohibit Provider from providing services to any other customer**.
>
> Provider acknowledges and agrees that the covenants in this Section 11 are given without any consideration beyond the fees payable under this Agreement ~~and are not tied to the disclosure or receipt of any confidential information, trade secret, or customer relationship~~ **, are ancillary to Provider's obligations under Section 6 (Confidentiality), and are supported by the disclosure of Customer's Confidential Information and the fees paid under this Agreement**.

**Comment.** High severity. As drafted the covenant is not ancillary to anything, expressly disclaims the only consideration that could support it, and restrains time (3 years), geography (all of Texas), and scope (any logistics company, and every one of Provider's employees) beyond what Texas permits. Texas will not save the customer from this: the court must reform the covenant, and reformation costs the customer damages for pre-reformation breach and leaves only injunctive relief. Rewriting it now preserves a workable restraint. [Tex. Bus. & Com. Code §§ 15.50(a), 15.51(c); Marsh USA Inc. v. Cook, 354 S.W.3d 764 (Tex. 2011)]

## R-2. Section 9.1 — Provider Indemnity

**Severity:** High
**Playbook rule:** IND-1, IND-2, IND-3
**Citation:** Ethyl Corp. v. Daniel Constr. Co., 725 S.W.2d 705 (Tex. 1987); Dresser Indus., Inc. v. Page Petroleum, Inc., 853 S.W.2d 505, 509 (Tex. 1993); Storage & Processors, Inc. v. Reyes, 134 S.W.3d 190 (Tex. 2004) (a clause failing fair notice is unenforceable as a matter of law)

**Original text (Section 9.1, the operative sentence sits in small plain type in the middle of the paragraph):**

> Provider shall defend, indemnify, and hold harmless Customer and its officers, directors, employees, agents, affiliates, and subcontractors from and against any and all claims, demands, actions, causes of action, damages, losses, liabilities, costs, and expenses, including reasonable attorneys' fees, arising out of or relating to this Agreement, the Services, or the acts or omissions of Provider or its Personnel, including without limitation claims for bodily injury, death, or property damage, and including without limitation any such claims to the extent caused by or arising out of the negligence, gross negligence, or other fault of Customer or its officers, directors, employees, or agents. Provider's obligations under this Section 9.1 are not limited by any insurance requirement in this Agreement.

**Proposed text:**

> **INDEMNITY FOR CUSTOMER'S OWN NEGLIGENCE.** Provider shall defend, indemnify, and hold harmless Customer and its officers, directors, employees, agents, affiliates, and subcontractors from and against any and all third-party claims, demands, actions, damages, losses, liabilities, costs, and expenses, including reasonable attorneys' fees, ~~arising out of or relating to this Agreement, the Services, or the acts or omissions of Provider or its Personnel, including without limitation claims for bodily injury, death, or property damage, and including without limitation any such claims~~ **to the extent arising out of** the negligent acts or omissions of Provider or its Personnel in the performance of the Services, **excluding any portion of such claims to the extent caused by the negligence or willful misconduct of Customer or its officers, directors, employees, or agents**. Provider's obligations under this Section 9.1 are not limited by any insurance requirement in this Agreement **and are supported by the insurance required by Section 12**.

**Comment.** High severity. The original asks Provider to indemnify Customer for Customer's own negligence, including gross negligence, in small plain text buried mid-paragraph. Under Texas law that intent must be expressed in specific terms and must be conspicuous; the buried formulation fails both requirements and is likely unenforceable as to Customer's own fault — which means the customer is buying an indemnity it may not be able to collect. The rewrite states the intent in a standalone, headed, conspicuous sentence, limits the indemnity to third-party claims caused by Provider, and restores comparative fault. Counsel should also confirm whether the Texas construction anti-indemnity statute applies to this maintenance work. [Ethyl Corp. v. Daniel Constr. Co., 725 S.W.2d 705 (Tex. 1987); Dresser Indus., Inc. v. Page Petroleum, Inc., 853 S.W.2d 505, 509 (Tex. 1993); Storage & Processors, Inc. v. Reyes, 134 S.W.3d 190 (Tex. 2004) (a clause failing fair notice is unenforceable as a matter of law); Tex. Ins. Code §§ 151.101, 151.102 `[VERIFY]` — applies only to construction contracts, including maintenance of an improvement to real property, where the indemnitor has insurance subject to the chapter]

## R-3. Section 18 — Governing Law; Venue

**Severity:** Medium-High
**Playbook rule:** GOV-1, GOV-2, GOV-3
**Citation:** DeSantis v. Wackenhut Corp., 793 S.W.2d 670 (Tex. 1990); In re AutoNation, Inc., 228 S.W.3d 663 (Tex. 2007); Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code § 15.020

**Original text (Sections 18.1–18.2):**

> This Agreement is governed by, and construed in accordance with, the laws of the State of Delaware, without regard to its conflicts of law principles.
>
> The exclusive venue for any action arising out of or relating to this Agreement is the state and federal courts located in New York County, New York, and each Party irrevocably consents to the personal jurisdiction of those courts and waives any objection based on venue or *forum non conveniens*, including any claim that New York is an inconvenient forum.

**Proposed text:**

> This Agreement is governed by, and construed in accordance with, the laws of the ~~State of Delaware~~ **State of Texas**, without regard to its conflicts of law principles. **Notwithstanding any other provision of this Agreement, Section 11 (Non-Competition) is governed by the laws of the State of Texas.**
>
> The exclusive venue for any action arising out of or relating to this Agreement is the state and federal courts located in ~~New York County, New York~~ **Harris County, Texas**, and each Party irrevocably consents to the personal jurisdiction of those courts and waives any objection based on venue or *forum non conveniens*, including any claim that ~~New York~~ **Harris County** is an inconvenient forum.

**Comment.** Medium-High severity. Two Texas parties, all work in Texas, and the only Texas-adjacent subject is a Texas non-compete: Delaware law and a New York forum buy litigation cost and a choice-of-law fight, and the non-compete will be measured against Texas's fundamental policy in any event. Texas generally enforces forum-selection clauses and has enforced an out-of-state forum clause even in a Texas non-compete dispute, so this is not a void clause — it is a bad and unnecessary one that will probably be enforced if signed. Strike it now. [DeSantis v. Wackenhut Corp., 793 S.W.2d 670 (Tex. 1990); In re AutoNation, Inc., 228 S.W.3d 663 (Tex. 2007); Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code § 15.020]

## R-4. Section 22.6 — Automatic Renewal

**Severity:** Medium
**Playbook rule:** REN-1
**Citation:** English v. Fischer, 660 S.W.2d 521 (Tex. 1983); Crim Truck & Tractor Co. v. Navistar Int'l Transp. Corp., 823 S.W.2d 591 (Tex. 1992)

**Original text (Section 22.6):**

> Upon expiration of the Initial Term, this Agreement automatically renews for successive two (2) year terms unless either Party gives the other Party written notice of non-renewal at least one hundred twenty (120) days before the end of the then-current term, in which case this Agreement expires at the end of that term. Notices of non-renewal must be sent to the addresses in Section 19 and are effective only if actually received. There is no obligation on either Party to remind the other of the renewal date or of the notice deadline. Fees for any renewal term continue at the then-current Monthly Fee unless the Parties agree otherwise in writing.

**Proposed text:**

> Upon expiration of the Initial Term, this Agreement automatically renews for successive ~~two (2) year~~ **one (1) year** terms unless either Party gives the other Party written notice of non-renewal at least ~~one hundred twenty (120) days~~ **sixty (60) days** before the end of the then-current term, in which case this Agreement expires at the end of that term. Notices of non-renewal must be sent to the addresses in Section 19 ~~and are effective only if actually received~~ **and are effective if sent in the manner required by Section 19 at least sixty (60) days before the renewal date**. ~~There is no obligation on either Party to remind the other of the renewal date or of the notice deadline.~~ **Provider shall send Customer a written reminder of the renewal date and the notice deadline at least ninety (90) days before the renewal date; if Provider does not, the notice deadline is extended to thirty (30) days after Provider actually sends the reminder.** Fees for any renewal term continue at the then-current Monthly Fee unless the Parties agree otherwise in writing **; either Party may terminate a renewal term for convenience on sixty (60) days' notice**.

**Comment.** Medium severity. No Texas statute governs automatic renewal between commercial parties, so this clause is enforced as written, and Texas will not imply a duty of good faith to rescue a missed deadline. On a two-year term the 120-day window falls in early July 2028; miss it and the customer is in for two more years at the then-current fee. Shortening the renewal to one year, cutting the notice window to 60 days, and adding a provider reminder makes the clause operable for a non-lawyer operations team. [English v. Fischer, 660 S.W.2d 521 (Tex. 1983); Crim Truck & Tractor Co. v. Navistar Int'l Transp. Corp., 823 S.W.2d 591 (Tex. 1992)]

## R-5. Section 10 — Limitation of Liability

**Severity:** High
**Playbook rule:** LOL-1, LOL-2
**Citation:** Dresser Indus., Inc. v. Page Petroleum, Inc., 853 S.W.2d 505 (Tex. 1993); Ethyl Corp. v. Daniel Constr. Co., 725 S.W.2d 705 (Tex. 1987)

**Original text (Sections 10.1–10.3):**

> Provider's aggregate liability arising out of or relating to this Agreement, whether in contract, tort, strict liability, or otherwise, is unlimited, and nothing in this Section 10 limits, reduces, or excludes Provider's liability for any claim, damage, or loss.
>
> Customer's total aggregate liability arising out of or relating to this Agreement, whether in contract, tort, strict liability, or otherwise, shall not exceed the Monthly Fee paid or payable in the one (1) month preceding the event giving rise to the claim (currently $18,000.00), regardless of the form of action, and this cap applies to all claims including claims for indemnity, gross negligence, and willful misconduct, and excludes only Customer's obligation to pay undisputed fees.
>
> Customer shall not be liable for any indirect, incidental, special, consequential, punitive, or exemplary damages, or for lost profits or lost business, however caused and on any theory of liability. This Section 10.3 does not apply to Provider, whose liability for such damages remains unlimited.

**Proposed text:**

> ~~Provider's aggregate liability arising out of or relating to this Agreement, whether in contract, tort, strict liability, or otherwise, is unlimited, and nothing in this Section 10 limits, reduces, or excludes Provider's liability for any claim, damage, or loss.~~ **Each Party's total aggregate liability arising out of or relating to this Agreement, whether in contract, tort, strict liability, or otherwise, shall not exceed the Monthly Fees paid or payable during the twelve (12) months preceding the event giving rise to the claim.**
>
> ~~Customer's total aggregate liability arising out of or relating to this Agreement, whether in contract, tort, strict liability, or otherwise, shall not exceed the Monthly Fee paid or payable in the one (1) month preceding the event giving rise to the claim (currently $18,000.00), regardless of the form of action, and this cap applies to all claims including claims for indemnity, gross negligence, and willful misconduct, and excludes only Customer's obligation to pay undisputed fees.~~
>
> Customer shall not be liable for any indirect, incidental, special, consequential, punitive, or exemplary damages, or for lost profits or lost business, however caused and on any theory of liability. ~~This Section 10.3 does not apply to Provider, whose liability for such damages remains unlimited.~~ **The limitations in this Section 10 apply equally to both Parties and do not apply to a Party's indemnification obligations under Section 9, breach of Section 6 or Section 7, infringement of intellectual property, fraud, gross negligence, or willful misconduct.**

**Comment.** High severity. An unlimited provider / $18,000 customer split, with the customer's cap expressly covering indemnity, gross negligence, and willful misconduct, is the most one-sided allocation Texas commercial drafting produces, and it contradicts Section 9. Texas enforces negotiated caps between sophisticated parties, but a clause that relieves a party of the consequences of its own negligence needs fair notice, and a cap that swallows the indemnity is internally inconsistent. The rewrite mirrors the cap at twelve months of fees and carves out the conduct Texas will not let a contract excuse. [Dresser Indus., Inc. v. Page Petroleum, Inc., 853 S.W.2d 505 (Tex. 1993); Ethyl Corp. v. Daniel Constr. Co., 725 S.W.2d 705 (Tex. 1987)]

## R-6. Section 4.2 — Net 90 Payment

**Severity:** Medium-High
**Playbook rule:** PAY-1
**Citation:** Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code §§ 38.001, 38.002; English v. Fischer, 660 S.W.2d 521 (Tex. 1983); Crim Truck & Tractor Co. v. Navistar Int'l Transp. Corp., 823 S.W.2d 591 (Tex. 1992)

**Original text (Section 4.2):**

> Amounts due under this Agreement are due and payable ninety (90) days after Customer's receipt of a correct and complete invoice (**Net 90**). Provider shall not charge interest, late charges, or collection costs on any amount not paid when due.

**Proposed text:**

> Amounts due under this Agreement are due and payable ~~ninety (90) days~~ **forty-five (45) days** after Customer's receipt of a correct and complete invoice (**~~Net 90~~** **Net 45**). ~~Provider shall not charge interest, late charges, or collection costs on any amount not paid when due.~~ **Undisputed amounts not paid when due bear interest at the lesser of one percent (1%) per month or the maximum rate permitted by law, from the due date until paid.**

**Comment.** Medium-High severity. Net 90 finances a quarter of the customer's operation at the provider's expense, and the ban on interest removes the only cheap incentive to pay. Attorney's fees are recoverable in Texas for services rendered and for breach of a written contract, subject to presentment, so the provider is not without a remedy — but the clause as drafted invites the breach. [Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code §§ 38.001, 38.002; English v. Fischer, 660 S.W.2d 521 (Tex. 1983); Crim Truck & Tractor Co. v. Navistar Int'l Transp. Corp., 823 S.W.2d 591 (Tex. 1992)]

## R-7. Section 4.3 — Unilateral Withholding

**Severity:** High
**Playbook rule:** PAY-1
**Citation:** English v. Fischer, 660 S.W.2d 521 (Tex. 1983); Crim Truck & Tractor Co. v. Navistar Int'l Transp. Corp., 823 S.W.2d 591 (Tex. 1992); Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code §§ 38.001, 38.002

**Original text (Section 4.3):**

> Customer may withhold payment of any amount, for any reason or for no reason, in Customer's sole and absolute discretion, and may set off against any invoice any amount that Customer claims, in its sole discretion, to be owed by Provider, without prior notice to Provider and without any obligation to state the basis for the withholding. Customer's exercise of its rights under this Section 4.3 is not a breach of this Agreement.

**Proposed text:**

> ~~Customer may withhold payment of any amount, for any reason or for no reason, in Customer's sole and absolute discretion, and may set off against any invoice any amount that Customer claims, in its sole discretion, to be owed by Provider, without prior notice to Provider and without any obligation to state the basis for the withholding. Customer's exercise of its rights under this Section 4.3 is not a breach of this Agreement.~~ **Customer may withhold only the specific portion of an invoice that it disputes in good faith, and only if it delivers written notice of the dispute within fifteen (15) days after receipt of the invoice, describing the basis for the dispute in reasonable detail. Undisputed amounts remain payable when due. Customer may set off only amounts that have been finally determined to be owed by Provider, whether by agreement in writing or by final, non-appealable order.**

**Comment.** High severity. This is the single most dangerous payment term in the draft. Texas implies no duty of good faith and fair dealing in an ordinary commercial contract, so "for any reason or for no reason" is exactly what it says, and the last sentence purports to make non-payment not a breach — which would also undercut the fee-recovery and attorney's-fee remedies the provider would otherwise have. [English v. Fischer, 660 S.W.2d 521 (Tex. 1983); Crim Truck & Tractor Co. v. Navistar Int'l Transp. Corp., 823 S.W.2d 591 (Tex. 1992); Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code §§ 38.001, 38.002]

## R-8. Section 7 — Service Data and AI Training

**Severity:** High
**Playbook rule:** DATA-1, DATA-2
**Citation:** Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code ch. 134A; Tex. Bus. & Com. Code ch. 541 (Texas Data Privacy and Security Act); Tex. Bus. & Com. Code chs. 551–554 (TRAIGA, H.B. 149, eff. Jan. 1, 2026); Tex. Bus. & Com. Code § 521.053

**Original text (Section 7.3):**

> Customer may, at its sole discretion and without restriction of any kind, use Service Data, and permit its affiliates, successors, and vendors to use Service Data, to train, develop, validate, fine-tune, and improve artificial intelligence and machine learning models and systems, including commercial and proprietary models, without any obligation to disclose such use to Provider, to obtain Provider's consent, to pay any additional fee, to anonymize or de-identify Service Data, or to limit such use in any respect. Provider waives any claim, objection, or restriction arising out of such use, including any claim based on trade secret, confidentiality, or data protection grounds.

**Proposed text:**

> ~~Customer may, at its sole discretion and without restriction of any kind, use Service Data, and permit its affiliates, successors, and vendors to use Service Data, to train, develop, validate, fine-tune, and improve artificial intelligence and machine learning models and systems, including commercial and proprietary models, without any obligation to disclose such use to Provider, to obtain Provider's consent, to pay any additional fee, to anonymize or de-identify Service Data, or to limit such use in any respect. Provider waives any claim, objection, or restriction arising out of such use, including any claim based on trade secret, confidentiality, or data protection grounds.~~ **Customer shall not, and shall not permit any affiliate, successor, vendor, or other third party to, use Service Data, in whole or in part, to train, develop, validate, fine-tune, or improve any artificial intelligence or machine learning model or system without Provider's prior written consent. Any permitted use must be documented in a written addendum that identifies the data sets, the purpose of the use, the retention period, the anonymization standard, and the deletion obligation, and that excludes Provider's Confidential Information, Provider's pricing and scheduling information, and any data that identifies Personnel. Customer shall notify Provider without unreasonable delay, and in any event within seventy-two (72) hours, after determining that Service Data has been subject to a breach of system security.**

**Comment.** High severity. Service Data is the provider's maintenance record: its methods, its pricing, its scheduling, its workforce. An unrestricted training right plus an express waiver of trade-secret and confidentiality claims is a transfer of the provider's competitive information, and it is inconsistent with Section 6. Texas protects that information through the Uniform Trade Secrets Act; the Texas AI statute restricts certain development and deployment of AI systems and imposes disclosure duties on deployers; and the provider remains responsible for breach notification for personal information it owns or licenses. The rewrite converts a blank check into a consented, documented, anonymized license. [Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code ch. 134A; Tex. Bus. & Com. Code ch. 541 (Texas Data Privacy and Security Act); Tex. Bus. & Com. Code chs. 551–554 (TRAIGA, H.B. 149, eff. Jan. 1, 2026); Tex. Bus. & Com. Code § 521.053]

## What was not redlined

- **Section 13 (Termination).** One-sided termination for convenience is a commercial allocation, not a Texas-law defect, and the playbook does not flag it.
- **Section 12 (Insurance), Section 14 (Records), Section 15 (Publicity).** No Texas baseline issue.
- **Section 6 (Confidentiality).** One-way in the customer's favor and short-tailed, but not one of the planted defects; the non-compete fix in R-1 re-anchors Section 11 to it. If the provider wants a mutual confidentiality clause, that is a separate ask.
- **Section 17.2 (Jury waiver).** Pre-suit contractual jury waivers are enforceable in Texas when made knowingly and voluntarily; conspicuous placement, a commercial setting and parties with counsel support that. The clause is acceptable as drafted if it is conspicuous. [In re Prudential Ins. Co. of Am., 148 S.W.3d 124 (Tex. 2004)]
