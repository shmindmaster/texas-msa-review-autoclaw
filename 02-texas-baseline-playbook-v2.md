# Texas Baseline Playbook — Bluebonnet / Pecan Creek MSA

**Scope.** Texas-law baseline for a business-to-business equipment-maintenance master services agreement in which both parties are Texas entities and all work is performed in Texas. The playbook states what the Texas baseline expects, not what this draft says.

**How to read it.** *Must* = the Texas baseline will not enforce the clause as drafted, or the clause creates a serious enforceability or compliance defect. *Should* = the Texas baseline disfavors the clause or makes it a poor risk allocation, but the clause is not automatically unenforceable. *Nice* = a drafting preference that improves the position but is not required.

**Citation discipline.** Only statutes and Texas Supreme Court decisions are cited as primary sources. Where a citation could not be confirmed as exact, or where the source does not squarely cover the point, `[VERIFY]` is marked and the point is sent to a licensed Texas attorney. No case name, statute section, or quotation is invented.

| **NCO-1** — Non-compete: enforceability threshold | Must | A covenant not to compete is enforceable only if it is ancillary to or part of an otherwise enforceable agreement, and only to the extent it limits time, geographical area, and scope of activity in a way that is reasonable and no greater than necessary to protect the promisee's goodwill or other business interest. A standalone restraint with no supporting obligation is not a Texas-enforceable covenant. | Tex. Bus. & Com. Code § 15.50(a) | "The covenants in this Section are ancillary to Provider's confidentiality obligations in Section 6 and to the fees payable under this Agreement." |
| **NCO-2** — Non-compete: reasonableness and reformation | Must | Time, geography, and scope must each be independently reasonable. A three-year, statewide bar on serving *any* logistics company, imposed on a company and its entire workforce, exceeds what § 15.50(a) permits. Texas does not void the covenant: the court *must* reform it to a reasonable restraint, but the promisee then gets only injunctive relief and may not recover damages for breach occurring before reformation. Overbreadth therefore costs the customer its damages remedy. The criteria and remedies in §§ 15.50, 15.501 and 15.51 are exclusive and preempt other law, including common law (§ 15.52, as amended in 2025 to add § 15.501). | Tex. Bus. & Com. Code §§ 15.50(a), 15.51(c), 15.52 (as amended by S.B. 1318, 89th Leg., eff. Sept. 1, 2025) | "For twelve (12) months after expiration or termination, Provider shall not, and shall cause its Personnel not to, use Customer's Confidential Information to provide equipment maintenance services to a known competitor of Customer with respect to the programs performed under this Agreement, within the counties in which Customer operates facilities under a Statement of Work." |
| **NCO-3** — Non-compete: consideration must support the restraint | Should | The covenant must be ancillary to an otherwise enforceable agreement. The consideration need not *give rise to* the interest being protected (the Texas Supreme Court rejected that test as not anchored in the statute), but it must be *reasonably related* to an interest the statute protects, such as goodwill or confidential information. An employer's promise to provide confidential information or training can supply that link once it is performed. An express disclaimer that the covenant is *not* tied to confidential information, trade secrets, goodwill or customer relationships removes the link the statute requires. Chapter 15 preempts common-law enforceability criteria, so the statute, not general restraint-of-trade doctrine, controls. | Marsh USA Inc. v. Cook, 354 S.W.3d 764 (Tex. 2011) (consideration "reasonably related" to a protectable interest; rejecting the "give rise" requirement of *Light*); Alex Sheshunoff Mgmt. Servs., L.P. v. Johnson, 209 S.W.3d 644 (Tex. 2006) (a promise that is performed later can support the covenant); Mann Frankfort Stein & Lipp Advisors, Inc. v. Fielding, 289 S.W.3d 844 (Tex. 2009) (an implied promise to provide confidential information, once performed); Light v. Centel Cellular Co. of Tex., 883 S.W.2d 642 (Tex. 1994) (ancillary requirement; partly superseded by *Sheshunoff* and *Marsh*); Tex. Bus. & Com. Code § 15.52 | Delete the "no additional consideration" acknowledgement and tie the covenant to Section 6 (Confidentiality) and to the fees paid under the Agreement. |
| **IND-1** — Indemnity for a party's own negligence must be express | Must | A party seeking indemnity from the consequences of its own negligence must express that intent in specific terms within the four corners of the contract. General, catch-all indemnity language does not shift the indemnitee's own negligence. | Ethyl Corp. v. Daniel Constr. Co., 725 S.W.2d 705 (Tex. 1987) | "…to the extent caused by the negligent acts or omissions of Customer," stated as its own sentence, not buried mid-paragraph. |
| **IND-2** — Indemnity: conspicuousness / fair notice | Must | Fair notice has two parts: the express negligence rule and conspicuousness. Conspicuousness is judged by the Uniform Commercial Code standard (contrast in size, bold type, capitals, heading) and whether both fair-notice requirements are met is a question of law for the court. Small, plain text in the middle of a long paragraph fails. A clause that fails fair notice is unenforceable as a matter of law unless the other party had actual knowledge of it, which is an affirmative defense. | Dresser Indus., Inc. v. Page Petroleum, Inc., 853 S.W.2d 505, 509 (Tex. 1993) (adopting the conspicuousness standard of Tex. Bus. & Com. Code § 1.201(10); compliance is a question of law); Storage & Processors, Inc. v. Reyes, 134 S.W.3d 190 (Tex. 2004) (a clause failing fair notice is unenforceable as a matter of law; actual knowledge is an affirmative defense) | Put the indemnity in its own section under an all-capitals heading, lead with a bold-capitals sentence ("PROVIDER SHALL INDEMNIFY CUSTOMER FOR CLAIMS TO THE EXTENT CAUSED BY THE NEGLIGENCE OF CUSTOMER"), and set the operative sentence in the same size and weight as the body text. |
| **IND-3** — Construction anti-indemnity statute | Should | Texas voids provisions in *construction* contracts (and agreements collateral to or affecting them) that require indemnification for claims caused by the indemnitee's own negligence or fault, its breach of a statute or regulation, or its breach of contract. The subchapter applies only to construction contracts for which the indemnitor is provided or procures insurance subject to the chapter, and it has exceptions (for example, claims for bodily injury or death of the indemnitor's own employees). "Construction contract" includes a contract for the "maintenance of … [an] improvement to or on public or private real property," so maintenance of equipment fixed to the warehouse (dock levellers, built-in conveyors, fire suppression) may qualify, while maintenance of movable equipment probably does not. Applicability here is unresolved and must go to counsel before the clause is signed. | Tex. Ins. Code §§ 151.001(5) (definition), 151.101 (applicability), 151.102 (void provisions), 151.103 and 151.105 (exceptions and exclusions) `[VERIFY` — whether the work under each SOW is maintenance of an improvement to real property`]` | Exclude, by name, "any portion of a claim to the extent caused by the negligence or willful misconduct of the indemnified party," and require the indemnity to be supported by the insurance in Section 12. |
| **GOV-1** — Choice of law | Should | Texas honors a contractual choice of law unless the chosen state has no substantial relationship to the transaction *or* application of the chosen law would violate a fundamental Texas policy. Texas's non-compete policy is such a policy: a foreign choice of law will not be applied to take away Texas non-compete protections. A Delaware choice of law for two Texas parties performing Texas work is therefore vulnerable at least as to the non-compete, and adds cost and uncertainty everywhere else. The override is not automatic: in *DeSantis* the court applied Restatement (Second) of Conflict of Laws § 187(2)(b) and found that Texas law would apply without the clause, that Texas had a materially greater interest, and that non-compete law is fundamental Texas policy. | DeSantis v. Wackenhut Corp., 793 S.W.2d 670 (Tex. 1990) | "This Agreement is governed by the laws of the State of Texas, without regard to its conflicts of law principles." If the other side insists on a foreign law clause, add: "Notwithstanding the foregoing, the covenants in Section 11 are governed by Texas law." |
| **GOV-2** — Venue and forum selection | Should | Forum-selection clauses are generally enforced in Texas and are given full effect unless the resisting party makes a strong showing to set them aside. In *AutoNation* the Texas Supreme Court enforced a Florida forum clause even in a Texas employee's non-compete dispute, so the governing-law point in GOV-1 does not defeat a forum clause. A New York County exclusive-venue clause is therefore likely to be enforced if signed: strike it in negotiation rather than relying on a court to refuse it. Separately, for a "major transaction" evidenced by a written agreement of at least $1,000,000, a defendant has a statutory right to transfer to a Texas county of proper venue if the agreement does not contain a venue clause. | In re AutoNation, Inc., 228 S.W.3d 663 (Tex. 2007); Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code § 15.020 | "The exclusive venue for any action arising out of or relating to this Agreement is the state and federal courts located in Harris County, Texas, and each Party consents to jurisdiction there." |
| **GOV-3** — Texas real-property construction overlay | Nice | Texas voids out-of-state governing-law and out-of-state venue provisions in construction contracts concerning Texas real property. Equipment maintenance of movable equipment is probably outside that statute, but if any Statement of Work covers built-in systems (dock levellers, conveyors fixed to the structure, fire suppression), the statute should be re-checked. | Tex. Bus. & Com. Code § 272.001 `[VERIFY` — confirm the definition of "construction contract" reaches the work described in the SOWs`]` | Texas governing law and Texas venue, which satisfies the statute if it applies. |
| **REN-1** — Automatic renewal notice | Should | No Texas statute governs automatic-renewal notice between commercial parties; the Texas automatic-renewal bills that have been filed target consumer subscription contracts and do not reach a B2B services agreement of this size. `[VERIFY]` The clause is therefore enforced as written, and Texas will not imply a duty of good faith to soften it. A 120-day, actually-received notice requirement on a two-year term is a trap: miss the window and the customer is locked in for two more years at the then-current fee. | English v. Fischer, 660 S.W.2d 521 (Tex. 1983) (Texas does not imply a covenant of good faith and fair dealing in every contract); Crim Truck & Tractor Co. v. Navistar Int'l Transp. Corp., 823 S.W.2d 591 (Tex. 1992) (no such duty absent a special relationship; a party may pursue its own interests) | "This Agreement renews for successive one (1) year terms unless either Party gives written notice of non-renewal at least sixty (60) days before the end of the then-current term. Provider shall send Customer a reminder notice at least ninety (90) days before the renewal date stating the renewal date and the notice deadline; failure to send the reminder extends the deadline to thirty (30) days after the reminder is actually sent." |
| **LOL-1** — Limitation of liability: balance and fair notice | Should | Sophisticated parties may cap liability, and Texas enforces negotiated caps. Two limits matter. First, a clause that relieves a party of liability for its own negligence needs fair notice (express negligence plus conspicuousness); the Texas Supreme Court has confined the fair-notice requirements to releases and indemnities for a party's own future negligence, and has declined to apply conspicuousness to a clause that allocates contract (economic) damages; it has not squarely decided whether ordinary limitation-of-liability clauses in services contracts need fair notice, and some courts of appeals have applied it to them. Confirm with counsel. `[VERIFY]` Second, a cap that is one-sided (provider unlimited, customer capped at a single month of fees, $18,000) and that purports to cap even gross negligence and willful misconduct is the allocation a Texas court is least likely to honor and the one that most damages the commercial relationship. | Dresser Indus., Inc. v. Page Petroleum, Inc., 853 S.W.2d 505 (Tex. 1993); Green Int'l, Inc. v. Solis, 951 S.W.2d 384 (Tex. 1997) (fair notice "explicitly limited to releases and indemnity clauses"; not applied to a no-damages-for-delay clause) `[VERIFY` — extension of the fair-notice rule to limitation-of-liability clauses in services contracts`]` | "Each Party's aggregate liability arising out of or relating to this Agreement is limited to the fees paid or payable in the twelve (12) months preceding the claim, except that this limit does not apply to a Party's indemnification obligations, breach of confidentiality, infringement, fraud, or gross negligence or willful misconduct." |
| **LOL-2** — Limitation of liability: mandatory carve-outs | Must | Whatever the cap, the clause must not purport to release a party from liability for its own fraud, willful injury, or (as drafted here) the indemnity it just promised. A customer cap of one month of fees that expressly applies to indemnity and gross negligence is internally inconsistent with Section 9 and invites a fight about which clause controls. | Ethyl Corp. v. Daniel Constr. Co., 725 S.W.2d 705 (Tex. 1987); Dresser Indus., Inc. v. Page Petroleum, Inc., 853 S.W.2d 505 (Tex. 1993) | "The limitation in this Section does not apply to liability arising from a Party's fraud, gross negligence, or willful misconduct, or from a Party's indemnification obligations under Section 9." |
| **PAY-1** — Payment terms and withholding | Must | A fixed monthly fee is an unconditional obligation. A clause permitting the customer to withhold "for any reason or for no reason" in its sole discretion converts that obligation into an option and is the kind of one-sided term Texas commercial law will not repair: Texas implies no duty of good faith and fair dealing in an ordinary commercial contract. The provider's remedies are breach of contract and statutory attorney's fees, which are recoverable for services rendered and for breach of a written contract, subject to presentment. Net 90 on a $18,000 monthly fee also finances the customer's operation at the provider's expense for a full quarter. | English v. Fischer, 660 S.W.2d 521 (Tex. 1983); Crim Truck & Tractor Co. v. Navistar Int'l Transp. Corp., 823 S.W.2d 591 (Tex. 1992); Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code §§ 38.001, 38.002; Tex. Fin. Code § 302.001(b) (general 10% ceiling, for the interest term in the fallback) `[VERIFY` — usury exposure of the 1% per month rate`]` | "Amounts due are payable within forty-five (45) days after Customer's receipt of a correct invoice (Net 45). Customer may withhold only the specific amount of an invoice that it disputes in good faith, and only if it gives written notice of the dispute within fifteen (15) days after receipt, describing the basis in reasonable detail. Undisputed amounts are paid when due. Overdue undisputed amounts bear interest at the lesser of one percent (1%) per month or the maximum rate permitted by law." |
| **PAY-2** — Statutory prompt-payment overlay | Nice | Texas's prompt-payment statute protects contractors and subcontractors on construction projects (payment by the 35th day after a written request, with interest). It is built on the definition of improving real property and most likely does not reach equipment maintenance of movable equipment, but a Statement of Work that includes built-in building systems should be checked. `[VERIFY]` | Tex. Prop. Code ch. 28 | Net 45 terms and a dispute carve-out, which keep the contract clear of the statute in any event. |
| **DATA-1** — Service data and AI training rights | Must | Unlimited rights to use the provider's service data to train AI systems, with no consent, no anonymization, no disclosure, and an express waiver of trade-secret and confidentiality objections, exceed any defensible license. Service Data includes the provider's maintenance methods, pricing, scheduling, and workforce information; unrestricted training use destroys the secrecy on which a trade-secret claim depends and is inconsistent with the confidentiality section. Texas's AI statute (effective January 1, 2026) restricts certain development and deployment of AI systems and imposes disclosure obligations on deployers; the privacy statute exempts individuals acting in a commercial or employment context, so employee and business-contact data in Service Data is largely outside it, but personal data of consumers captured in the data is not. `[VERIFY]` | Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code ch. 134A (Texas Uniform Trade Secrets Act), including §§ 134A.002 (definitions), 134A.003 (injunctive relief), 134A.004 (damages); Tex. Bus. & Com. Code ch. 541 ("Consumer Data Protection," enacted as the Texas Data Privacy and Security Act, H.B. 4, 88th Leg.), § 541.001(7) ("consumer"); Texas Responsible Artificial Intelligence Governance Act (H.B. 149, 89th Leg., effective January 1, 2026), codified as Tex. Bus. & Com. Code Title 11, Subtitle D, chs. 551–554, including § 551.001 ("artificial intelligence system") and § 552.001 ("deployer," "developer") | "Customer shall not, and shall not permit any third party to, use Service Data to train, develop, validate, or improve any artificial intelligence or machine learning model without Provider's prior written consent. Any permitted use shall be documented in a written addendum identifying the data sets, the purpose, the retention period, anonymization standards, and deletion obligations, and shall not include Provider's Confidential Information or any data that identifies Personnel." |
| **DATA-2** — Breach notification allocation | Must | A party that conducts business in Texas and owns or licenses personal information must notify affected individuals of a breach of system security, and must notify the Texas Attorney General for a breach affecting 250 or more Texas residents. `[VERIFY]` A contract that lets the customer feed service data into AI systems without controls leaves the provider unable to meet its own notification and security duties. | Tex. Bus. & Com. Code § 521.053 | "Each Party shall notify the other of any breach of system security affecting the other Party's data without unreasonable delay and in any event within seventy-two (72) hours after determination that a breach occurred, and shall reasonably cooperate in any notification required by law." |
| **SF-1** — Statute of frauds | Must | A promise or agreement that is not to be performed within one year is not enforceable unless in writing. A two-year term with automatic two-year renewals must be in writing, and amendments must be too. | Tex. Bus. & Com. Code § 26.01 | Keep the writing requirement and the signed-amendment clause; do not accept oral or click-through amendments. |

## Notes on what this playbook deliberately does not say

- **No outcome prediction.** Nothing above forecasts who wins a dispute. It states what the Texas baseline requires and where the draft departs from it.
- **No Texas Supreme Court decision squarely governs** limitation-of-liability clauses in a pure services contract (the closest are *Dresser* and *Green Int'l v. Solis*, 951 S.W.2d 384 (Tex. 1997); some courts of appeals have applied fair notice to such clauses), or the application of the construction anti-indemnity statute to equipment maintenance. Those two gaps are flagged `[VERIFY]` and belong with a licensed Texas attorney.
- **No consumer statute is cited as applicable.** Texas automatic-renewal legislation is consumer-oriented; none was located that reaches this B2B agreement. `[VERIFY]`
- **Two further Texas anti-indemnity overlays are not analysed in detail.** Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code ch. 127, "Indemnity Provisions in Certain Mineral Agreements" (often called the Texas Oilfield Anti-Indemnity Act), applies to agreements pertaining to oil, gas, or water wells and almost certainly does not reach equipment maintenance for a warehousing customer, but counsel should confirm. `[VERIFY]` The construction overlay is row IND-3 above.
- **Federal law is out of scope** except as it is unavoidable (for example, the federal Defend Trade Secrets Act immunity notice, 18 U.S.C. § 1833(b), which should be considered for any confidentiality clause and is flagged for counsel). `[VERIFY]`
