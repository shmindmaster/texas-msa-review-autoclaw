# Brief: Texas MSA review package (fictional, for a product demo)

Everything in this task is fictional. It is a product demo and test fixture for a Texas commercial-contract review tool. It is not legal advice, and no real client, matter or person is involved.

Work in a single project folder only.

## Your role

You are a careful Texas commercial-contracts reviewer helping a non-lawyer operations lead at a small Texas company. You flag issues, cite the Texas source for each one, and suggest fallback language. You never predict outcomes, and you send anything uncertain to a licensed Texas attorney.

## Step 1: Draft the fictional contract

Draft a master services agreement of about 4–6 pages:

* Provider: Bluebonnet Field Services LLC, a fictional Texas LLC in Austin that provides equipment maintenance technicians.
* Customer: Pecan Creek Logistics, Inc., a fictional Texas corporation in Houston that runs warehouses.
* Fee: about $18,000 a month. Term: 2 years.

Plant these problems in the draft, written the way a customer-favorable form really would be:

1. A non-compete: the provider's technicians may not work for any logistics company anywhere in Texas for 3 years after the agreement ends. It is not tied to any confidential information or other consideration.
2. A broad indemnity: the provider indemnifies the customer for all claims, including those caused by the customer's own negligence. The wording is in small, plain text in the middle of a long paragraph.
3. Delaware governing law and exclusive venue in New York County, New York, though both parties and all work are in Texas.
4. Automatic renewal for 2-year terms unless either party gives notice 120 days before expiry, buried in the boilerplate.
5. One-sided liability: the provider's liability is unlimited, and the customer's is capped at one month of fees.
6. Net 90 payment, with the customer free to withhold payment for any reason.
7. A clause letting the customer use the provider's service data to train AI systems, with no limits or disclosure.

## Step 2: Texas baseline playbook

Write a playbook table with these columns: Rule | Must / Should / Nice | What the Texas baseline expects | Primary source citation | Fallback language.

Cover at least:

* non-competes (Texas Business and Commerce Code §§15.50–15.52)
* indemnity for a party's own negligence (the express negligence doctrine and fair notice requirements from Texas Supreme Court case law)
* governing law and venue
* automatic renewal notice
* limitation of liability balance
* payment terms
* data and AI use

Cite primary sources only: statutes, rules and Texas Supreme Court cases. If you are not certain a citation is exact, write `[VERIFY]` next to it. Never invent a case name, statute section or quote.

## Step 3: Redline

Redline only the clauses the playbook flags. For each change, show the original text, the proposed text, and a comment with the severity, the playbook rule and the citation.

## Step 4: Risk memo for the operations lead

Write one to two pages in plain English:

* a short summary
* a table of material issues: issue, severity (High / Medium / Low), why it matters in Texas, recommended action, citation
* a section titled "Needs a licensed Texas attorney" for anything uncertain or high-stakes
* a closing note that this is not legal advice

## Step 5: Self-audit

Before you finish, check every citation in the playbook, redline and memo. List each one in a final table with these columns: Citation | Where used | Confidence (High / Medium / Low) | Notes. Mark anything you could not confirm as `[VERIFY]`.
